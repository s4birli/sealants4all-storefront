When you're fixing something heavy back to a wall, you've got two families to choose from: **mechanical fixings** (expansion bolts, sleeve and wedge anchors) and **chemical fixings** (resin-bonded anchors). Pick the wrong one and you either crack the substrate or end up with an anchor that won't hold load. Here's how to choose.

## How each one works

**Mechanical fixings** hold by **friction and expansion**. You drill, insert the anchor, and tightening it expands a sleeve or wedge that grips the side of the hole. The grip comes from pressure against the base material.

**Chemical fixings** hold by **bond**. You drill, clean the hole, inject a two-part resin, then push in a threaded stud or rebar. The resin cures and keys into every pore of the substrate, locking the stud in place with no expansion stress.

## The deciding factor: your base material

This is what usually settles it.

| Base material | Best choice | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solid dense concrete | Either | Both perform well; mechanical is quick |
| Hollow block / brick | **Chemical** | Resin fills the voids; expansion anchors can blow out hollow walls |
| Cracked or near-edge concrete | **Chemical** | No expansion force to split the substrate |
| Soft / aerated masonry | **Chemical** (with sleeve) | Friction anchors won't grip |
| Quick, light-duty into solid | **Mechanical** | Faster, no cure time |

The golden rule: **expansion (mechanical) anchors push outward**, so near an edge or in hollow/cracked material they can crack or fail. **Chemical anchors apply no expansion stress**, which is why they win for edge fixings, hollow substrates and the heaviest loads.

## When to use chemical (resin) anchors

- High or safety-critical loads — structural steel, balustrades, canopies.
- Fixing close to an edge or between two close fixings.
- Hollow brick, blockwork or perforated masonry.
- Rebar connections and post-installed dowels.
- Where you need a gas-tight, water-tight set.

## When mechanical fixings make sense

- Solid, sound concrete with room around the fixing.
- Light-to-medium loads where speed matters.
- Temporary fixings you may need to remove.
- No cure time available — load it the moment it's tight.

## Don't forget the install details

Even the right anchor fails if you skip the basics:

- **Drill the correct diameter and depth** — under-drilling kills resin bond and over-drilling kills mechanical grip.
- **Clean the hole** — for chemical anchors, blow and brush out dust twice; resin bonds to the substrate, not to drilling dust.
- **Respect edge and spacing distances** — manufacturers publish minimums for a reason.
- **Mind the cure temperature** — resin cure time stretches in the cold.
