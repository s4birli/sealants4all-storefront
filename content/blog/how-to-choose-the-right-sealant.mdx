Picking the wrong sealant is one of the most expensive mistakes on site. A bead that cracks, peels or grows mould inside six months means a return visit, a reputation hit and wasted material. This guide walks through the four questions that decide which sealant you actually need — so you order the right cartridge the first time.

## 1. How much will the joint move?

Every sealed joint moves as materials expand and contract. The headline spec to check is **Movement Accommodation Factor (MAF)** — the percentage of joint width a sealant can flex without failing.

- **Low movement (±5–10%)** — internal frames, skirting, decorative gaps. A general-purpose acrylic or low-modulus silicone is fine.
- **Medium movement (±20–25%)** — windows, door frames, expansion joints. Reach for a quality neutral-cure silicone or an MS polymer.
- **High movement / structural (±25%+)** — curtain walling, façade joints, flooring. Use a high-performance polyurethane or MS polymer rated for the job.

If you're sealing a moving joint with a rigid filler, it *will* split. Match the chemistry to the movement.

## 2. Where is it — wet, sunny, or buried?

Environment narrows the field fast:

- **Bathrooms & wet rooms** — you need a **sanitary silicone** with a fungicide to resist mould. Browse our [joint sealing range](/category/joint) for sanitary grades.
- **Kitchens & food areas** — neutral-cure, low-odour, mould-resistant silicone.
- **External façades & expansion joints** — UV-stable, weatherproof. MS polymers and PU sealants from [Sika](/search?q=Sika) and [Soudal](/search?q=Soudal) are the trade standard.
- **Below ground or constantly wet** — look at [waterproofing materials](/category/water) rather than a standard sealant.

## 3. Do you need to paint over it?

This single question rules out half the market. **Silicone cannot be overpainted** — paint beads off it. If the joint will be decorated, you need an **acrylic decorator's caulk** or a **paintable MS polymer / hybrid**. Get this wrong and the painter is back on the phone the next day.

## 4. What are you sticking it to?

Adhesion depends on the substrate:

| Substrate | Best choice |
| --- | --- |
| Glass, glazed tile, sanitaryware | Neutral silicone |
| Porous stone, concrete, brick | MS polymer or PU (often with a [primer](/category/primers)) |
| Plastics & composites | MS polymer (test first) |
| Metal & cladding | PU or MS polymer |

For demanding or porous substrates, a [primer or activator](/category/primers) dramatically improves the bond and long-term durability. Don't skip it on structural work.

## Quick reference

- **Bathroom/kitchen** → sanitary neutral silicone
- **Windows/doors** → neutral silicone or MS polymer
- **External movement joints** → MS polymer or polyurethane
- **Paint over it** → acrylic caulk or paintable hybrid
- **Wet/below ground** → dedicated waterproofing system
- **Fire-rated penetrations** → intumescent [fire protection](/category/fire) sealant only

## The trade shortcut: MS polymers

If you want one cartridge that covers most jobs, **MS (modified silane) polymers** are the modern all-rounder: high movement, paintable, bonds to nearly everything wet or dry, no primer on most substrates, and low odour. They cost a little more than basic silicone but save you carrying five different tubes.
