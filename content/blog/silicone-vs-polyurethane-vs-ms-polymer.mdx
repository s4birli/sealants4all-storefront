Three sealant chemistries dominate the trade: **silicone**, **polyurethane (PU)** and **MS polymer** (also called hybrid or modified silane). They look identical in the cartridge but behave very differently once cured. Here's how they stack up — and when to reach for each.

## At a glance

| | Silicone | Polyurethane | MS Polymer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Movement | High | High | Very high |
| Paintable | ❌ No | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes |
| UV resistance | Excellent | Poor–Fair | Excellent |
| Adhesion (no primer) | Limited | Good | Excellent |
| Bonds when damp | ❌ | Limited | ✅ |
| Sandable cured | ❌ | ✅ | Partly |
| Odour | Higher (acetic) | Solvent | Low |
| Cost | £ | ££ | ££–£££ |

## Silicone — the glazing & wet-room specialist

Silicone is the king of **glass, sanitaryware and weatherproofing**. It stays flexible for decades, shrugs off UV and is the only realistic choice for showers, baths and glazing.

**Strengths:** outstanding UV and temperature resistance, excellent on non-porous surfaces, sanitary grades resist mould.

**Weaknesses:** you **cannot paint over it**, adhesion to porous substrates is poor without a primer, and acetic-cure types smell strong and can corrode some metals (use neutral cure near metal and mirrors).

**Use it for:** bathrooms, kitchens, windows, glazing, [joint sealing](/category/joint) on glass and tile.

## Polyurethane — the tough, structural workhorse

PU sealants cure into a **tough, abrasion-resistant, sandable** bead with serious grab. They're a favourite for flooring, construction joints and anywhere the seal takes mechanical knocks.

**Strengths:** very strong adhesion, paintable, sandable, great for high-traffic and structural joints.

**Weaknesses:** poor UV stability (it chalks and yellows in sun unless painted), shorter shelf life, solvent odour.

**Use it for:** floor expansion joints, construction and concrete joints, bonding where toughness matters. [Sika](/search?q=Sika) and [Soudal](/search?q=Soudal) PU grades are the trade benchmark.

## MS Polymer — the modern all-rounder

MS polymers combine the best of both: silicone's **UV resistance and flexibility** with PU's **adhesion and paintability** — minus the downsides. They bond to damp surfaces, most substrates without a primer, are low-odour and isocyanate/solvent-free.

**Strengths:** very high movement, paintable, UV-stable, bonds to almost anything (even wet), low odour, safe near metals.

**Weaknesses:** costs more, and not as glass-clear or as hard-wearing underfoot as a dedicated silicone or PU.

**Use it for:** external façade joints, mixed-substrate work, bonding-and-sealing in one, and as a single do-it-all cartridge on the van.

## So which should you buy?

- **Sealing glass, tiles or a wet room?** → Silicone.
- **Tough floor or structural joint that takes a beating?** → Polyurethane.
- **One cartridge for most external and mixed-material jobs?** → MS polymer.
- **Needs painting?** → PU or MS polymer (never silicone).
- **Fire-rated penetration?** → none of the above — use a dedicated [fire protection](/category/fire) sealant.
